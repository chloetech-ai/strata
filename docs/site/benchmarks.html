Benchmarks

First overall when one system has to do it all

Applications need full text, filters, SQL, vectors and hybrids together. We test all of them at once, against thirteen search engines and databases. ChloeDB is not the fastest at everything, and this page shows where.

Updated 7 November 2026 · measured, never edited

Faster hybrid search than SQLite, DuckDB or LanceDB with every system on one thread (an older ChloeDB build, four BEIR collections); 4.8× to 33× with ChloeDB on up to eight threads, on all five.

ChloeDB's place on every kind of query

First or close to it on vectors, hybrids, facets and SQL aggregates. Behind the specialists on typos and key lookups.

How much faster or slower, engine by engine

ChloeDB's speed over each engine's, on the kinds both run. At 1M it keeps pace with Qdrant and Milvus at one client and is 1.6 to 9 times as fast as the rest; at 16 clients it serves 1.8 to 28 times their queries a second. At 10M it is about twice as fast as Elasticsearch, OpenSearch and Vespa. Open an engine to see each kind, including the ones where ChloeDB is slower.

Where another system is clearly faster

The large losses, and what is being done about each.

Key lookups

10M · 0.99 ms, 5th of 9; Postgres 0.37

Postgres, Weaviate, ParadeDB and Qdrant read a row by id in under half ChloeDB's time, and Redis, on a bigger node, in a fifth of it. Most of ChloeDB's time is the hop from a coordinator to the shard over gRPC, not the lookup. #299 answers a pod's own shard inside its coordinator: 34% more lookups a second at 16 clients on the same nodes, already in these numbers. A lighter shard protocol would close more of the gap.

Typo-tolerant search

10M · 45.8 ms; Elasticsearch 34.6

A misspelt word is expanded against the dictionary. #242 and #266 took it from 824 to 45.8 ms at 10M and from 120 to 11.9 at 1M, but Elasticsearch is still faster at 10M, and at 1M Typesense and Meilisearch take 6 ms. Next: a deletion index (SymSpell), in memory first.

BM25 × recency

10M · 8.89 ms, 3rd of 9; Vespa 5.42

Vespa and Qdrant are 1.5 to 1.6 times faster at one client at 10M, and at 1M Qdrant takes 1.95 ms against ChloeDB's 4.00. Planned, not started: skip the blocks whose recency bound cannot reach the top 10.

BM25 at 16 clients

10M · 625 q/s; OpenSearch 720

ChloeDB answers a single BM25 query the fastest at 10M (3.56 ms), but under load OpenSearch and Elasticsearch serve 15% and 9% more. The shards' own scoring is what waits: moving the coordinators into the shard pods (#287) added 3% on the same nodes, and both layouts hit their CPU limits in bursts.

One client, through the coordinator

1M · BM25 2.03 ms on 6 November's main; 1.64 before #287

A client reaches a coordinator in a shard pod across the network, as with Elasticsearch: 4 to 29% slower at one client at 1M, 7% at 10M (median over the kinds), for up to 1.9 times the queries a second at 16 clients.

Vector recall at shared settings

10M · recall@10 0.791; Qdrant 0.941

ChloeDB scores 8-bit codes and re-scores the best 40, so read its vector speeds with this recall. At 1M, asking for 256 candidates instead of 64 lifts it from 0.809 to 0.931, past Qdrant's 0.905, and ChloeDB still serves 11% more queries a second; with a filter, 3.6 times as many at about equal recall. The sweep.

Short graph hops

Graph · 1 hop 0.35 ms over gRPC; Memgraph 0.18

Memgraph and FalkorDB, both in memory, answer one to three hops in about half ChloeDB's time. ChloeDB's own time on one hop is 0.10 ms against Memgraph's 0.02; gRPC instead of HTTP JSON took 0.1 ms off the rest. Not fixed yet.

Indexed price updates in an app

571/s on main; SQLite 7,670

Updating a row's price while it carries a text and a vector index is 2.4 times slower than on ChloeDB's 2 November build (1,389 a second) and 13 times slower than SQLite. Found by the 6 November rerun, in both of its writes runs; not fixed yet. Without the indexes the same update got faster (3,276 a second).

Five fixes, measured on the cluster

On 7 November an image with five pull requests, all now merged, ran on the same nodes as 6 November's main, serving the same data, three passes each. The rankings on these pages use it, labelled 7 Nov. Key lookups gain the most. At 1M most other kinds serve 5 to 13% more queries a second at 16 clients (hybrids and SQL aggregates 1 to 3%, typos 2% fewer), and no median at one client moves by more than 5%.

  • #299 answers a pod's own shard inside its coordinator. Key lookups at 16 clients: 9,210 to 12,673 a second at 1M, 8,967 to 11,993 at 10M.
  • #293 starts a merged partition's vector graph from its inputs' graphs. The 10M shards' merges take 40 and 58 minutes instead of 54 and 77; the whole 1M build 336 s instead of 470.
  • #294 runs the merger below the queries (nice 10), and #295 stops stream reads from waiting on a publish of every write batch.
  • #296 sweeps the vector candidates (ef) for ChloeDB and Qdrant: recall against speed.

1M on two m-4vcpu-32gb nodes (Xeon Platinum 8168), 7 November; 10M in its report. Images b022e39 (main of 6 November) and 861ab1d (main at 8e66622 with #293, #294, #295, #296 and #299). Raw data: rerun-1107-do. The 5 November fixes, measured the same way: bigger-items-do.

How we keep it fair

The same resources

Same CPUs, memory, data and vectors for every engine. At 1M every engine ran on 4 November, each lane's engines taking turns on the same machines; an engine whose code or settings changed since, ChloeDB among them, ran again on machines of the same size, and the rankings use its newest run. At 10M each engine had machines of its own.

Answers checked

Recall@10 against exact search, and nDCG@10 where there are judgments.

Medians, not the best run

The median of three passes where there were three.

Every gap shown

A kind an engine cannot run counts as last place, and failed runs are reported.

Load not ranked

ChloeDB's shards were built offline, so load times are shown but not ranked.

Reproducible

Scripts, settings, data hashes and raw results are all in the repository.

Questions

Are we biased?

Probably. We build ChloeDB and wrote every engine's queries. Each engine runs at its defaults plus the tuning its documentation recommends, every setting is in the method, and we audited our own runs. If you know a better configuration, open an issue and we will run it again.

Why sum places instead of averaging speeds?

Latencies differ a thousandfold between kinds of query, so an average is decided by the slowest kind. Places weigh every test the same.

Doesn't counting a missing kind as last favour ChloeDB?

It favours breadth, which a mixed workload needs. At 1M ChloeDB also leads on the seven kinds every engine runs; at 10M Postgres leads on the four every engine answered (33 points against ChloeDB's 42). Each report shows its ranking under five rules.

Why only nine engines at 10M?

Five did not run in 52 GiB. Milvus and Redis then ran with twice the memory: shown, not ranked.

Can I run it myself?

Yes: How we test has the commands for a laptop or a cloud account.
