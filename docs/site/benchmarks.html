First overall when one system has to do it all

Applications need full text, filters, SQL, vectors and hybrids together. We test all of them at once, against thirteen search engines and databases. Strata is not the fastest at everything, and this page shows where.

Where another system is clearly faster

The large losses, and what is being done about each.

Typo-tolerant search

10M · 43.9 ms; Elasticsearch 34.6

A misspelt word is expanded against the dictionary. #242 and #266 took it from 824 to 43.9 ms at 10M and from 120 to 13.2 at 1M, but Elasticsearch is still faster at 10M, and at 1M Typesense and Meilisearch take 6 ms.

BM25 at 16 clients

10M · 604 q/s; OpenSearch 720

Moving the coordinators into the shard pods (#287) added 3% on the same nodes. The shards' own scoring is what waits, and both layouts hit their CPU limits in bursts. The 5 November image served 584 here and 591 on Elasticsearch's and OpenSearch's nodes, so the gap is not the machines.

Key lookups

10M · 1.02 ms, 6th of 9; Postgres 0.37

Postgres, Weaviate, ParadeDB and Qdrant read a row by id in under half Strata's time. Most of Strata's time is the hop through a coordinator, now in a shard pod across the network. Under load the new layout helps: 9,159 a second at 16 clients, up from 5,529 on the same nodes.

One client, through the coordinator

1M · BM25 2.03 ms on main; 1.64 before #287

A client now reaches a coordinator in a shard pod across the network, as with Elasticsearch: 4 to 29% slower at one client at 1M, 7% at 10M (median over the kinds), for up to 1.9 times the queries a second at 16 clients.

Vector recall at shared settings

10M · recall@10 0.795; Qdrant 0.941

Strata scores 8-bit codes and re-scores the best 40, so read its vector speeds with this recall.

Indexed price updates in an app

571/s on main; SQLite 7,670

Updating a row's price while it carries a text and a vector index is 2.4 times slower than on Strata's 2 November build (1,389 a second) and 13 times slower than SQLite. Found by this rerun, in both of its writes runs; not fixed yet. Without the indexes the same update got faster (3,276 a second).

How we keep it fair

The same resources

Same CPUs, memory, data and vectors for every engine. In the latest runs, engines take turns on the same machines.

Answers checked

Recall@10 against exact search, and nDCG@10 where there are judgments.

Medians, not the best run

The median of three passes where there were three.

Every gap shown

A kind an engine cannot run counts as last place, and failed runs are reported.

Load not ranked

Strata's shards were built offline, so load times are shown but not ranked.

Reproducible

Scripts, settings, data hashes and raw results are all in the repository.

Questions

Are we biased?

Probably. We build Strata and wrote every engine's queries. Each engine runs at its defaults plus the tuning its documentation recommends, every setting is in the method, and we audited our own runs. If you know a better configuration, open an issue and we will run it again.

Why sum places instead of averaging speeds?

Latencies differ a thousandfold between kinds of query, so an average is decided by the slowest kind. Places weigh every test the same.

Doesn't counting a missing kind as last favour Strata?

It favours breadth, which a mixed workload needs. At 1M Strata also leads on the seven kinds every engine runs; at 10M Postgres leads on the four every engine answered (33 points against Strata's 44). Each report shows its ranking under five rules.

Why only nine engines at 10M?

Five did not run in 52 GiB. Milvus and Redis then ran with twice the memory: shown, not ranked.

Can I run it myself?

Yes: How we test has the commands for a laptop or a cloud account.
