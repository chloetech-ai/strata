Compared with others

What usually takes several systems

Search engines, vector databases and embedded databases each cover part of what an app with search and recommendations needs. Here is ChloeDB beside each, row by row, and where they are ahead.

Elasticsearch is shown at its default Basic licence, which the benchmarks run. Hover a mark for its detail.

Why one engine

Nothing to keep in step

Text, columns, vectors and edges are written in one transaction. No second store to re-index.

Planned together

The planner sees the filter, the words, the vector and the graph walk of one statement.

Same on a laptop and a cluster

A shard is a ChloeDB file, and every step answers the same SQL.

Where others are ahead

Look at these before choosing ChloeDB.

Elasticsearch and OpenSearch

Years at very large scale, a large ecosystem and analyzers for dozens of languages. Faster on typo search (Elasticsearch 34.6 ms against ChloeDB's 45.8 at 10M) and on BM25 under load (OpenSearch 720 queries a second at 16 clients against 625).

Vespa

Proven at very large scale, multi-phase tensor ranking and a managed cloud. Fastest on BM25 × recency at 10M.

Milvus, Qdrant and Weaviate

Indexes for billions of vectors, on disk or GPUs, and managed clouds. Qdrant's recall@10 at 10M is 0.941, ChloeDB's 0.791 at the shared settings.

SQLite and PostgreSQL

Decades of hardening. SQLite reads a row by id in 8 µs against ChloeDB's 21; Postgres looks up keys in about a third of ChloeDB's time at 10M (0.37 against 0.99 ms).

Tantivy

Faster facet counts on small collections, and faster cold range filters.

ChloeDB itself

A young project: analyzers for 22 languages, new and less tried than Lucene's, no managed service, no point-in-time recovery yet, and one writer per file.
