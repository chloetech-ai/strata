No server · one file in an app · 2, 3 and 6 November 2026

One file in your app, against what apps embed today

Apps keep rows in SQLite, analytics in DuckDB, vectors in LanceDB or FAISS and search in Tantivy. ChloeDB does all of it in one file. Here it runs as a Python library against each, one at a time on an idle 8-vCPU node. ChloeDB on main ran again on 6 November, beside its 2 November build on the same node; the others ran on 2 November.

First on search, hybrid search, recommendations and reads beside a writer. Behind SQLite on single-row work and price updates, and DuckDB on aggregates and the heavy join.

Search

Hybrid search fastest on every collection

Hybrid search is 4.8 to 33× faster than the next system (1.9 to 17× held to one thread, an older build), with nDCG@10 within 0.02 of the best. Vectors are 18 to 24× faster at recall@10 0.977 to 0.997. BM25 is now fastest on all five collections, though on the two smallest its 1.1× lead over Tantivy is inside the node's margin. On the same node, main's medians are 1.04 to 2.8× faster than its 2 November build's on these kinds; its 99th percentile got slower on the three larger collections, for BM25 (1.2 to 1.6×) and filtered hybrid (up to 1.8× on quora). Under each bar: nDCG@10, or recall@10 for vectors.

Tantivy kept stopwords the others dropped, which is most of its nDCG lead on quora; with the same stopwords they tie (below). The others, and the one-thread column, ran on 2 November's node, where the 2 November build searched about 5% slower than in its column here.

The app

Pages and joins faster than SQLite; single rows slower

ChloeDB is 7 to 10× faster than SQLite at filtered, sorted pages, about 5× at the heavy join and at reads beside a writer, and 3.4× and 180× at the two aggregates. SQLite reads one row in 8 µs, against ChloeDB's 21 µs by key. DuckDB is fastest at aggregates and the heavy join. Compare writes through the 2 November build's column: this node commits single rows faster.

Writes and streams

Indexed writes, and reads beside a stream

With a text and a vector index, ChloeDB commits single rows 3.0 to 3.2× faster than SQLite and 9 to 15× faster than DuckDB and LanceDB; LanceDB takes 10,000-row batches fastest. Title and vector updates are now 26 and 36× the 2 November build's; price updates are 2.4× slower than that build's on the same node, and 13× slower than SQLite's. While 10,000 rows a second stream in, ChloeDB answers 8,781 reads a second, 9 to 16× DuckDB's and SQLite's.

Both slowdowns of main showed in both writes runs: price updates with the indexes on (571 and 641 a second, against 1,389 and 1,379), and reads while 50,000 rows a second stream in (89 and 160 a second, against 3,190 and 3,227, while main writes 1.7 times as many rows). Without indexes, price updates got faster (3,276 a second against 2,268).

Recommendations

Similar items in under a millisecond, under filters

ChloeDB answers similar items in 0.51 ms, against FAISS beside SQLite's 0.57, and at the 99th percentile in 0.91 against 8.5; LanceDB takes 25× longer. ChloeDB's home feed fuses vectors with a PageRank walk in one statement: 4.87 ms, 20× its 2 November build, and 5.8× faster than LanceDB's with the walk in app code.

Against Tantivy

Tantivy as the app's store, and BM25 side by side

On a newer node (Xeon Gold 6548N) with ChloeDB of 3 November, not run again: ChloeDB commits single rows and updates 30× faster and reads rows faster; Tantivy aggregates, joins and bulk-loads faster. With the same stopwords their BM25 ties on nDCG@10.

Where ChloeDB is behind

Single rows, aggregates, price updates and a fast stream

  • Single rows. SQLite reads one by id in 8 µs against ChloeDB's 30 through SQL (21 by key), and joins a user's reviews in 22 µs against 465.
  • Aggregates. DuckDB counts reviews per brand in 5.2 ms and per month in 10.1, against ChloeDB's 29.3 and 19.2 (SQLite 99.5 and 3,569). DuckDB's heavy join takes 215 ms, ChloeDB's 524.
  • Price updates. 3,276 a second without indexes against SQLite's 6,640 at FULL, and 571 with them against 7,670; the latter was 1,389 on the 2 November build.
  • Reads beside a fast stream. 89 to 160 a second while 50,000 rows a second arrive, down from 3,190.
  • Tail latency on larger collections. BM25's 99th percentile is 1.2 to 1.6× its 2 November build's on fiqa, trec-covid and quora, and filtered hybrid's 1.8× on quora (one run).
