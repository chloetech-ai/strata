Cluster · 1M passages · DigitalOcean · 4 to 7 November 2026

Fourteen engines, 1M passages

ChloeDB's 7 November image, main with five fixes that are now merged, against thirteen engines, with the same data, the same 14 kinds of query and three passes each. Each engine's numbers come from its newest run: an engine whose code or settings changed ran again, the others kept their 4 November numbers. Engines that shard had two 4-vCPU machines, the others one 8-vCPU machine, with the same CPU limit.

ChloeDB is first under all five ranking rules. It runs all 14 kinds and is in the top half on 42 of 45 tests; its lowest places are key lookups, 10th of 14 at the median and 13th at the 99th percentile. It is fastest outright on 9 tests: the three of SQL aggregates, both hybrids at one and four clients, and facets at the 99th percentile and at four clients. Qdrant is faster on BM25, Typesense and Meilisearch on typos, and Redis on key lookups and vectors.

Recall against speed

At equal recall, more queries a second than Qdrant

The rankings use 64 candidates (ef) for every engine, where ChloeDB's vector recall is 0.809 and Qdrant's 0.905. On 7 November both ran at 64, 128 and 256. At 256 ChloeDB passes Qdrant's default recall (0.931) and still serves 11% more queries a second at 16 clients. With a filter it serves 3.6 times as many at about equal recall and is faster at one client at every setting. Without a filter Qdrant answers a single query sooner: 1.84 ms at recall 0.905, against ChloeDB's 2.97 at 0.931.

ChloeDB on two m-4vcpu-32gb nodes (two pods of 3.4 CPUs), Qdrant on one m-8vcpu-64gb node, Xeon Platinum 8168, 7 November; medians of three passes.

Writes and streams

Searchable in 7 ms, and a stream written once

ChloeDB and Vespa make a write searchable when it returns (7.1 and 8.6 ms); Elasticsearch, OpenSearch and Solr refresh every second. At 16 clients ChloeDB's single inserts are faster than Elasticsearch's and OpenSearch's and a little slower than Solr's, and its updates are more than twice as fast as any other's, because it sets one column in place. From one client ChloeDB's writes are slower than on 4 November (307 inserts a second against 417, 384 updates against 547): other nodes, and a coordinator now across the network; the runs did not separate the two. After a consumer crash, ChloeDB resumes exactly, since offsets commit with the rows; the others write some events twice. None lost any.

Load and size

Shown, not ranked

ChloeDB's shards were built offline from local files, the others over HTTP, so load times do not compare.

Where ChloeDB is behind

Three kinds where it is clearly slower

  • Typo search. 11.9 ms, down from 120 in the first run (#242, #266); Typesense and Meilisearch take 6.0, Elasticsearch 14.7.
  • Key lookups. 1.03 ms through a coordinator, 10th of 14; Redis takes 0.09 and Postgres 0.21. Half the keys are the coordinator's own shard's and skip the network (#299), but the median key is one of the other half.
  • BM25 × recency. 4.00 ms, 4th; Qdrant takes 1.95. Plain BM25 is 5th at 2.38 ms, behind Qdrant's 1.63.
Since the first run

SQL aggregates 22 times faster, typos ten times, BM25 95% more at 16 clients

ChloeDB's medians in the first 1M run (3 November) and in the 7 November image. The coordinator caches each shard version's term statistics (#245), so a BM25 query takes one round to the shards instead of two; a misspelt word is no longer expanded against the whole dictionary (#242, #266); aggregates read their groups from the columns (#252, #264); and later BM25 segments start at the scores the earlier ones found (#258). The machines changed too: Elasticsearch's BM25 median went from to ms, OpenSearch's from to . One-client latency also pays the network hop to a coordinator in a shard's pod (#287), which the first run's coordinator, on the client's machine, did not.
