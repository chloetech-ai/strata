Cluster · 10M passages · DigitalOcean · 3 to 6 November 2026

Ten times the data, the same budget

The 1M run with ten times the data. Every engine had its own DigitalOcean machines with 6.8 CPUs and 52 GiB, and nine of the fourteen finished. Strata on main ran again on 6 November, Elasticsearch and OpenSearch on 5 November with their result caches off; the others' numbers are from 3 November.

Strata is fastest on 26 of the 45 tests and in the top half on 41. Its lowest places are key lookups (6th of 9) and recall under a filter. The SQL aggregate that was its worst result on 3 November (4.3 s) now takes 36.7 ms, the fastest of the run, and typo search 43.9 ms instead of 824. Head to head with Elasticsearch and OpenSearch it is fastest on 37 of the 45 tests (below).

Latest main · 5 and 6 November 2026

Head to head with Elasticsearch and OpenSearch

Strata on main of 6 November (image b022e39), and Elasticsearch and OpenSearch of 5 November with their result caches off, each on two m3-4vcpu-32gb machines, three passes each. Of the three, Strata is fastest on 37 of the 45 tests. Elasticsearch is fastest on typos, OpenSearch on BM25 under load (720 queries a second at 16 clients, Strata 604) and on key lookups. The two days had different machines: on Strata's, its 5 November image served 0.75 to 1.10 of its 5 November queries a second at 16 clients, so where the others lead under load, part of it may be the machines.

Raw data: Strata's rerun and 5 November's run. The rest of this page ranks every engine from its newest run.

The 128 GB machine

Milvus and Redis, with more memory

Both needed more than 52 GiB, so they ran with the same CPUs on 128 GB and are not ranked. Redis is fastest on vectors (at recall 0.684) and key lookups, and takes 0.8 to 1.5 s on text. Milvus has the best filtered recall, at about an eighth of Strata's vector throughput. While searching on 3 November, Redis held 72 GiB, Milvus 64 and Strata 2.2.

Writes and streams

Searchable in 7 ms

Strata and Vespa make a write searchable when it returns (7.1 and 10.0 ms); Elasticsearch and OpenSearch refresh every second (820 and 674 ms at the median). At 16 clients Strata's updates are more than three times as fast as theirs (5,408 a second against 1,516 and 1,567), because it sets one column in place. From one client its writes are slower than on 4 November (328 inserts a second against 405), on other machines and with the coordinator across the network. A crashed stream consumer resumes exactly, since offsets commit with the rows. Solr has no write numbers.

Load and size

Shown, not ranked

Strata's shards were built offline from local files, the others over HTTP, so load times do not compare.

Where Strata is behind

Typos, key lookups, recall and BM25 under load

  • Typo search. 43.9 ms, against Elasticsearch's 34.6 (OpenSearch 46.6). It took 824 ms on 3 November's build, before #242 and #266.
  • Key lookups. 1.02 ms, against 0.37 to 0.47 for Postgres, Weaviate, ParadeDB and Qdrant: most of it is the hop through a coordinator.
  • Vector recall. 0.794 at the shared settings, against Qdrant's 0.941 and Postgres's 0.826; 0.819 under a filter, against Solr's 0.917.
  • BM25 × recency. 8.55 ms, against Vespa's 5.42 and Qdrant's 5.86.
  • BM25 at 16 clients. 604 queries a second, against OpenSearch's 720 and Elasticsearch's 683. A coordinator in each shard pod (#287) added 3% on the same machines, where 5 November's image served as many BM25 queries as on 5 November's: the shards' own scoring is what waits.
What did not finish

Five engines that did not run at 52 GiB

  • Typesense ran out of memory at 7.2M documents.
  • Milvus ran out of memory building its index, and ran again on 128 GB.
  • Meilisearch had indexed about 4M documents after nine hours.
  • Manticore was still merging after eight hours.
  • Redis keeps everything in memory, so it ran on 128 GB from the start.
  • Postgres finished, but its text search averaged 28 s a query on a 107 GB table; with a 20 s limit, its text kinds count as not answered.

Strata's first attempt hit a 5 s server deadline on cold vector queries and was run again with the client's 30 s (#244). No number here comes from a failed attempt.
