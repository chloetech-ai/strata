Cluster · 10M passages · DigitalOcean · 3 to 7 November 2026

Ten times the data, the same budget

The 1M run with ten times the data. Every engine had its own DigitalOcean machines with 6.8 CPUs and 52 GiB, and nine of the fourteen finished. ChloeDB ran again on 7 November with five fixes that are now merged, Elasticsearch and OpenSearch on 5 November with their result caches off; the others' numbers are from 3 November.

ChloeDB is fastest on 25 of the 45 tests and in the top half on 41. Its lowest places are key lookups (6th of 9) and recall under a filter. The SQL aggregate that was its worst result on 3 November (4.3 s) now takes 36.8 ms, the fastest of the run, and typo search 45.8 ms instead of 824. Head to head with Elasticsearch and OpenSearch it is fastest on 38 of the 45 tests (below).

Head to head · 5 and 7 November 2026

Head to head with Elasticsearch and OpenSearch

ChloeDB's 7 November image (861ab1d, with the five fixes), and Elasticsearch and OpenSearch of 5 November with their result caches off, each on two m3-4vcpu-32gb machines, three passes each. Of the three, ChloeDB is fastest on 38 of the 45 tests. Elasticsearch is fastest on typos, and OpenSearch on BM25 under load (720 queries a second at 16 clients, ChloeDB 625), key lookups at four clients and recall under a filter. The days had different machines: on 6 November's, ChloeDB's 5 November image served 0.75 to 1.10 of its 5 November queries a second at 16 clients, so where the others lead under load, part of it may be the machines.

Raw data: ChloeDB's rerun and 5 November's run. The rest of this page ranks every engine from its newest run.

Five fixes, now merged · 7 November 2026

Key lookups 34% faster under load

6 November's main and the 7 November image on the same two m3-4vcpu-32gb machines, serving shards built from the same data, three passes each. #299 answers a pod's own shard inside its coordinator, so half the keys skip the network: 8,967 to 11,993 lookups a second at 16 clients. Other kinds gain up to 9% (typos lose 3%), less than at 1M, since the shards' own work is a larger part of each query. With #293 the shards' merges take 40 and 58 minutes instead of 54 and 77.

Engine by engine

About twice as fast as the closest engines

ChloeDB's speed over each engine's, the geometric mean over the kinds both run. Postgres answered five kinds at this size, so its ratio rests on those. Milvus and Redis ran on a 128 GB machine. Open an engine for each kind.

The 128 GB machine

Milvus and Redis, with more memory

Both needed more than 52 GiB, so they ran with the same CPUs on 128 GB and are not ranked. Redis is fastest on vectors (at recall 0.684) and key lookups, and takes 0.8 to 1.5 s on text. Milvus has the best filtered recall, at about a seventh of ChloeDB's vector throughput. While searching on 3 November, Redis held 72 GiB, Milvus 64 and ChloeDB 2.2.

Writes and streams

Searchable in 7 ms

ChloeDB and Vespa make a write searchable when it returns (7.6 and 10.0 ms); Elasticsearch and OpenSearch refresh every second (820 and 674 ms at the median). At 16 clients ChloeDB's updates are more than three times as fast as theirs (5,001 a second against 1,516 and 1,567), because it sets one column in place. From one client its writes are slower than on 4 November (289 inserts a second against 405), on other machines and with the coordinator across the network. A crashed stream consumer resumes exactly, since offsets commit with the rows. Solr has no write numbers.

Load and size

Shown, not ranked

ChloeDB's shards were built offline from local files, the others over HTTP, so load times do not compare.

Where ChloeDB is behind

Typos, key lookups, recall and BM25 under load

  • Typo search. 45.8 ms, against Elasticsearch's 34.6 (OpenSearch 46.6). It took 824 ms on 3 November's build, before #242 and #266.
  • Key lookups. 0.99 ms, against 0.37 to 0.47 for Postgres, Weaviate, ParadeDB and Qdrant: most of it is the hop from a coordinator to the shard. #299, already in these numbers, skips it for the coordinator's own shard, which helps under load more than at one client.
  • Vector recall. 0.791 at the shared settings, against Qdrant's 0.941 and Postgres's 0.826; 0.821 under a filter, against Solr's 0.917. With 256 candidates ChloeDB reaches 0.903 and 0.916, at 630 and 555 queries a second at 16 clients (ef.md).
  • BM25 × recency. 8.89 ms, against Vespa's 5.42 and Qdrant's 5.86.
  • BM25 at 16 clients. 625 queries a second, against OpenSearch's 720 and Elasticsearch's 683. A coordinator in each shard pod (#287) added 3% on the same machines, where 5 November's image served as many BM25 queries as on 5 November's: the shards' own scoring is what waits.
What did not finish

Five engines that did not run at 52 GiB

  • Typesense ran out of memory at 7.2M documents.
  • Milvus ran out of memory building its index, and ran again on 128 GB.
  • Meilisearch had indexed about 4M documents after nine hours.
  • Manticore was still merging after eight hours.
  • Redis keeps everything in memory, so it ran on 128 GB from the start.
  • Postgres finished, but its text search averaged 28 s a query on a 107 GB table; with a 20 s limit, its text kinds count as not answered.

ChloeDB's first attempt hit a 5 s server deadline on cold vector queries and was run again with the client's 30 s (#244). No number here comes from a failed attempt.
