Each design met its main target and missed at least one secondary one. Together they hold every point on 4 of the 10 frontiers measured, and Loom is on all ten.

Pyramid: a smaller Roaring, with faster unions on clustered docIDs

Met: 24–33% smaller than Roaring, faster unions on the clustered collection (1.08×) and on selective queries (1.31×), level on dense ones, and faster conjunctions on clustered and selective queries.

Missed: unions on uniform and WikiText (13–14% more time), and conjunctions on uniform (42% more), WikiText (87% more) and dense queries (24% more). There, probes into mid-density sparse blocks cost more than Roaring's gallop through one contiguous array.

Loom: PEF's size with bitmap-like speed

Met: on every collection, PEF's size with 2.4–3.0× faster conjunctions and 3.8–7.7× faster unions. It sits on all ten frontiers and has the fastest conjunction on the clustered collection, on dense queries and for three- to six-term queries.

Missed: the stretch goal of beating Roaring everywhere. On uniform and WikiText, Loom takes 21–54% more time than Roaring on AND, and on all three collections 34–74% more on OR. Roaring is 1.7–1.9× Loom's size.

Entropic: the smallest index on disk

Met: the smallest serialized index on every collection, 11–14% below interpolative coding and 6–15% below PEF, while answering queries 2.0–4.7× faster than interpolative coding.

Missed: it is not smaller in memory. Decode tables add 1.3–22 MB, which puts it above PEF on clustered and WikiText. Its conjunctions are 1.7–1.9× slower than PEF's, and builds 7–10× slower.

Which one to use

  • Loom as the default. It replaces PEF outright, and takes at most 1.54× Roaring's conjunction time at 53–58% of its size.
  • Pyramid where unions over clustered docIDs dominate or memory is less tight, as in faceting, filters or OR-heavy candidate generation. On uniform and WikiText lists Roaring unites faster.
  • Entropic for cold or storage-bound tiers, where bytes on disk or over the network matter more than query time. It needs decode tables built on demand before it saves memory too.

Limitations

  • One machine. A MacBook Air with an Apple M2 (4 performance and 4 efficiency cores, 24 GB, macOS 26.0.1), on mains power. Two independent runs of the same codecs agree to 0.3% at the median and within 3% in all but one cell (12%), so differences under 5% (Pyramid against Roaring on dense OR, Loom against Pyramid at two terms) are not significant. The study first ran on a 4-core cloud VM with an Emerald Rapids Xeon and AVX-512, where Pyramid had the fastest union on every workload; on the M2, Roaring unites faster on uniform and WikiText lists.
  • NEON kernels. Every SIMD kernel has an AVX-512 version for x86-64, a NEON version for aarch64 and a portable twin; these numbers time the NEON builds. NEON has 128-bit registers and no compress or gather instructions, so some kernels take a different route than their AVX-512 versions (Entropic's decoder runs four blocks' state machines in scalar registers), and a few stay scalar where NEON was not faster.
  • Tuned on these data, on the Xeon. Thresholds such as 29 values per dense block, the 1.6× Flat bias and Loom's \(\lambda = 5/4\) were chosen while profiling these collections on the VM and kept for the M2, so the margins may be smaller on other data and machines.
  • Boolean, static, single-threaded. No scoring, top-k or block-max metadata, no updates, one core. Queries come from the same generator as the first round rather than a real query log, also on WikiText.

Next steps

  • Pyramid: a contiguous u16 form for chunks made mostly of mid-density sparse blocks, so that a probe is one search instead of a mask, a directory entry and a byte scan. This targets the WikiText conjunction gap.
  • Loom: per-partition block-max scores for dynamic pruning. Its 16-posting grid already gives the boundaries.
  • Entropic: build decode tables lazily per list or share parametric models, so memory falls below PEF as well as disk.
