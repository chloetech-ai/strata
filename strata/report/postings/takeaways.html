1. Choose the skip structure first, the gap coder second. Conjunctions spend their time in NextGEQ, so how a codec jumps matters more than how fast it decodes. Elias-Fano's select-based jumps made PEF the fastest compressed list codec for AND on every collection, although it decodes 1.3–1.5× slower than BP128. Uncompressed arrays, galloping through plain memory, are still 1.3–2.1× faster than PEF on the M2 (on the Xeon VM that first ran this study, PEF matched or beat them).
  2. PEF is the balanced default. It was the smallest or within 1% of the smallest codec on all three collections (4.75–8.08 bits per posting on query lists) and its AND time was 2.0–3.8× Roaring's. Interpolative coding matches its size but is 3.7–4.3× slower.
  3. Roaring is the speed choice, especially for unions. Container-wise operations with SIMD kernels gave the fastest AND on every collection and 4.8–11× faster OR than the best compressed list codec, because ORing words beats merging docIDs one at a time. The price is 1.6–1.9× PEF's space on query lists, and more on vocabularies with many short lists. Its AND advantage shrinks as queries get more selective (2.5× at two terms, 1.6× at six).
  4. Use SIMD only where sizes are similar. A NEON 8×8 block-compare kernel is 6.8× faster than a scalar merge on equal-size lists, but real conjunctions are skewed, and galloping wins beyond about a 1:35 size ratio on the M2. CRoaring's 64× switch point was measured on older hardware and should be re-tuned per machine.
  5. Reorder docIDs. Clustering cut the size of PEF, interpolative coding, Roaring and the tries by 32–50% and roughly halved conjunction time for list codecs. Plain Elias-Fano cannot benefit; partitioning is what lets it.
  6. Compact tries are compact, not fast, in this implementation. On clustered data they are smaller than BP128 and Roaring, but 2.8–3.3× slower than PEF for AND on every query length, and they pay heavily for short lists (23 bits per posting over the whole WikiText vocabulary). The paper measured lists of at least 4,096 postings on URL-sorted web collections; the queries here pair short lists with very long ones, where cursor skipping is hard to beat.
  7. Implementation quality changes the ranking. Two Roaring implementations with byte-identical containers differ by 3.0× on AND and 6.5× on OR. Published comparisons between formats should be read with that spread in mind.

Limits of these measurements. One MacBook Air with an Apple M2 (4 performance and 4 efficiency cores, 24 GB, macOS 26.0.1), on mains power, single-threaded, timed with the NEON kernels. Two independent runs of the same codecs agree to 0.3% at the median and within 3% in all but one cell (12%). The study first ran on a 4-core Emerald Rapids Xeon VM with AVX-512; the conclusions that changed on the M2 are noted above. Queries are generated from co-occurring terms rather than taken from a real query log. All operations are Boolean, with no scoring or top-k pruning, and every result is written out as a Vec<u32> unless marked "count only". Gov2 or ClueWeb in ds2i format, with real TREC queries, can be run with --source ds2i.
