1. Choose the skip structure first, the gap coder second. Conjunctions spend their time in NextGEQ, so how a codec jumps matters more than how fast it decodes. Elias-Fano's select-based jumps made Elias-Fano or PEF the fastest list codec for AND on every collection, matching or beating uncompressed arrays, although they decode 2.3–2.6× slower than BP128.
  2. PEF is the balanced default. It was the smallest or within 1% of the smallest codec on all three collections (4.75–8.08 bits per posting on query lists) and its AND time was 1.5–3.1× Roaring's. Interpolative coding matches its size but is 4–5× slower.
  3. Roaring is the speed choice, especially for unions. Container-wise operations with SIMD kernels gave the fastest AND on every collection and 4.5–10× faster OR than the best list codec, because ORing words beats merging docIDs one at a time. The price is 1.6–1.9× PEF's space on query lists, and more on vocabularies with many short lists. Its AND advantage shrinks as queries get more selective (1.9× at two terms, 1.3× at six).
  4. Use SIMD only where sizes are similar. Block-compare kernels are 4.4× faster than a scalar merge on equal-size lists, but real conjunctions are skewed, and galloping wins beyond about a 1:24 size ratio on this CPU. CRoaring's 64× switch point was measured on older hardware and should be re-tuned per machine.
  5. Reorder docIDs. Clustering cut the size of PEF, interpolative coding, Roaring and the tries by 32–50% and roughly halved conjunction time for list codecs. Plain Elias-Fano cannot benefit; partitioning is what lets it.
  6. Compact tries are compact, not fast, in this implementation. On clustered data they are smaller than BP128 and Roaring, but 2.6–3.5× slower than PEF for AND on every query length, and they pay heavily for short lists (23 bits per posting over the whole WikiText vocabulary). The paper measured lists of at least 4,096 postings on URL-sorted web collections; the queries here pair short lists with very long ones, where cursor skipping is hard to beat.
  7. Implementation quality changes the ranking. Two Roaring implementations with byte-identical containers differ by 2.9× on AND and 7.5× on OR. Published comparisons between formats should be read with that spread in mind.

Limits of these measurements. One 2.1 GHz Xeon VM (AVX-512, no VP2INTERSECT), single-threaded. The clustered collection was run three times end to end, and individual timings varied by up to about 15% between runs; the conclusions above hold in every run. Queries are generated from co-occurring terms rather than taken from a real query log. All operations are Boolean, with no scoring or top-k pruning, and every result is written out as a Vec<u32> unless marked "count only". Gov2 or ClueWeb in ds2i format, with real TREC queries, can be run with --source ds2i.
